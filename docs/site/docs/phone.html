On your phone

The Life Is app is on its way to the app stores. This page says how it will work.

Two ways to use the app

When the app first opens it asks where your household should live:

  • On this phone. Everything is kept on the phone, encrypted. No server, no account.
  • On a Life Is you run. The app is a window onto the Life Is on your own computer.

You can change your mind later. Nothing held in either place is touched.

When the trial ends, nothing is hidden or deleted. Those parts become read-only: you can still see everything and make a backup.

Reaching a Life Is at home

A Life Is on your home computer answers only that computer unless you say otherwise. Two good ways to reach it from your phone:

  • On your home network. Let it answer other devices on your network (see Install), and open it at your computer's address while you are at home.
  • From anywhere, privately. A private network such as Tailscale joins your phone and your computer so the phone can reach it wherever you are, without opening it to the internet.

Giving it a web address of its own is possible too, and Install can set that up. Then it is on the public internet, behind your password.

The camera

Scanning barcodes and stickers uses the camera. The picture is read on the device and goes nowhere. In a browser, the camera only works over https or on the computer itself.

Short requests

The app answers short requests from a shortcut on its icon, from Siri or from Google: add to the grocery list, note some spending, ask what is for dinner. Anything that adds waits for a tap, so a link alone can never change anything.
