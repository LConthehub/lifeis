Your home

The tidy section holds what you own, where it is, what it is for, and where it has been. It also holds the records that go with each thing, your pets, your licenses and a House Manual for a sitter. Jobs that come round again are in Upkeep. Its tabs are Things, Places, Upkeep, Pets, Licenses, House Manual and Home services.

Places

A place is a room, a shelf in it, a bin on the shelf. Write a whole path at once, with › or > between the levels: Garage › Tool shelf › Red bin. Levels that already exist are reused.

Wherever Life Is asks "Where it is", the list ends with A new place…, so you never have to stop to make one.

Things, and kinds of things

You make your own kinds (Fabric, Tools, Board games) and give each the fields you care about: a fabric's width, a game's number of players. Ready-made sets get you started, and every part of them can be changed.

The ready-made Hunting and shooting set adds Firearms, Ammunition and Hunting gear, and is off until you add it. Guns and ammunition are private to you: nobody else can see them unless you pick them on the gun's page, and they stay out of search, the Home page, the home inventory and the assistant. List of guns on the Things page prints your guns with their serial numbers and photos.

Each thing has a page of its own with a photo, where it is, what it is for, its records, and its history.

Your own fields

Kinds give every thing of that kind the same fields. When one thing needs something no other has, choose Change on the thing and use Add a field of your own. Give it a name and a value, such as "Paint color: Sage". It shows on that thing's page, and search finds it.

Asset numbers

Each thing gets an asset number when you add it, such as 000-012. The numbers count up from the last one. The number shows on the thing's page and in the home inventory, and it is printed on the thing's sticker. The Find box on the Things page finds a thing by its number.

Insured

On a thing, check Insured if your insurance covers it. The home inventory can then be limited to just those things.

Things you no longer have

When you sell something, give it away, throw it out, or it is stolen, open it and choose No longer have it. Pick what happened and the day. You can say who it went to, and for a sale, what you got. The thing leaves your lists and totals, but its record is kept. To see those things, choose No longer have on the Things page.

Changed your mind, or marked the wrong one? Open it and choose I have it again.

Bringing in from HomeBox

If you keep your things in HomeBox, export its inventory as a CSV file. On Things, choose Bring in from HomeBox and pick the file. The file is read on your own Life Is and sent nowhere. You see what is in it first: how many things, which kinds, places and tags are new, which things will go under "No longer have", and any asset numbers that were already taken and will get the next free number. Nothing is kept until you say so. Bringing in the same file again adds nothing.

Filling a place

Open a place and choose Fill this place. Then keep scanning:

You scanWhat happens
A book, film, album or game you do not haveIt is looked up, added, and put here
Something you already haveIt is moved here, and its history says where from
A blank Life Is stickerIt asks what the sticker is on, once
Another place's stickerIt switches to filling that place
A barcode nobody knowsIt asks what it is, and keeps the barcode for next time

No barcode? Type the name and press Enter.

Looking a barcode up asks an outside catalog, so it is off until you turn it on. It sends the number under the barcode, and nothing else.

Stickers

Life Is prints small stickers with a code your camera reads.

  • Place stickers go on a shelf, a box or a bin. Scan one to see what is inside.
  • Thing stickers go on things with no barcode of their own. Print a sheet of blank ones, stick them on, and say what each is on when you scan it. A sticker for a thing you already have shows its name and its asset number.

Only someone signed in to your Life Is sees anything. To anyone else a sticker is an address that asks them to sign in.

History

Every thing remembers when it came in, each time it moved and where from, and each time it was used and what for. Renaming a room later does not rewrite the past.

What it is for

A thing can belong to activities: the sewing machine is for Sewing, the drill for DIY and Home maintenance. Activities gathers the gear for each, the projects of it, and its upkeep.

Packing

For moving house or putting things away, Packing makes numbered boxes in one go. Pack each by scanning, and search will tell you which box anything is in. A box unpacks into its new home in one step.

Receipts, serial numbers and warranties

After a fire, a flood or a theft, an insurer asks what you had, what it was worth, and whether you can show it. These records answer all three, and build up as you use Life Is.

Open a thing and find its Records card. It can keep:

  • when and where it was bought, and what it cost
  • what it is worth now, if that is different
  • its model and serial number
  • when its warranty ends

Beside the records, keep the thing's papers: a receipt, the manual, a warranty card. Each is a PDF or a photo, up to 12 MB. A photo is made smaller before it is kept; a PDF is kept as it is.

Search finds a thing by its serial or model number, which is what a repair shop asks for on the phone. A warranty about to end appears in your morning reminder a month ahead, a week ahead, the day before and on the day, so there is time to claim.

If a thing is a car, Life Is can also look for safety recalls on it. See Licenses and recalls.

The home inventory

On the Things page, Home inventory lists everything room by room, with what each is worth, its serial number, and whether there is a photo and a receipt to show for it. It says how many things still have no value, so you know what is left to do.

Before you print, choose what goes in it: all rooms or only the ones you pick, only things worth at least an amount, things with no value or not, Only insured things, photos, and one room per page. Download as a spreadsheet (CSV) gives a row for each thing with its room, model, serial number, purchase, cost, worth, warranty, asset number and whether it is insured. Photos and papers are not in the spreadsheet.

Press Print or save as PDF, and keep a copy somewhere other than home. An inventory that burns with the house helps nobody.

Pets

Each pet has a profile: what they are, their breed and birthday, their vet with a number you can tap to call, their microchip number, what they eat, and notes such as allergies. Add a photo, and note their weight from time to time: Life Is shows the change since last time.

A pet's care is upkeep that belongs to them. Pick from what suits their kind (heartworm prevention for a dog, the litter box for a cat) or add anything else. Say when it was last done and Life Is knows when it is next due. Care works like any other job in Upkeep: it shows in your morning reminder and on the calendar, and each time it was done is kept. Monthly meds belong here.

A pet can also have a day-to-day routine, such as feed, fresh water and walk. It shows on every person's front page, and anyone can tick a step so everyone sees who did it and when. See Routines for a pet.

The House Manual

The House Manual is what someone else needs to know to look after your home: a sitter, a guest, family, or you on the day something goes wrong. It has four parts, each with ideas to start from:

  • In an emergency: where the water and gas shut off, the breaker box
  • Who to call: the plumber, a neighbor with a key
  • How the house works: the Wi-Fi, the thermostat, the alarm
  • Day to day: trash days, plants, bedtimes

An entry can say where a thing is, using the places you already keep, and carry a phone number to tap. On a phone, under Who to call, tap Choose from your contacts to fill in a name and number. Only the one contact you tap is read, and Life Is asks for no access to your address book. Your pets appear by themselves, with their food and vet.

Press Print and put it in a drawer. Or use Share it by a link, for a sitter. The link opens the manual on their phone with no sign-in. You choose how long it works: a day, a week, or until you turn it off. Making a new link stops the old one.

Mark an entry private, such as an alarm code, and it is never on the shared link. The shared page is plain: nothing on it can be changed, and it shows nothing else of your Life Is.

Home services

Home services is a tab next to the House Manual. It holds links to the programs you run at home: a media server, a photos app, the page for your router. Tap a link and it opens in a new tab (in the phone app, in the phone's own browser).

The person in charge adds and changes the links. Each one has a name, an address, a color and an icon from the set Life Is already uses, and a note. You can add a second address and say which works at home and which works away; the link then has a button for each. You can also name a group (Watch, Files, House) and give an order.

The person in charge picks who sees each link: everyone, chosen people, or only me. A link you cannot see is not there for you at all: not in the tab, not in search, not in your download. Each person can pin the links they use. Pinned links show on a Home services card on your Home page, which you can hide like any other card.

Life Is only opens an address when you tap it. It does not look up logos or check whether a program is running. There is no place to keep a password, and an address with a name and password in it is refused: sign in on the page itself.

Mark a link Show to guests and it is on the shared House Manual too, with only the address that works away from home. A link that only has a home address says it only works at home. Home services are in the download, and come back with Bring it in (the person in charge does that).
