Your data

Nobody else holds your household. There is no Life Is account and no Life Is cloud.

Where it is kept

  • In the phone app: in an encrypted database on the phone. The key that opens it is kept in the phone's own secure storage.
  • On a computer you run: in an encrypted database in the folder you chose, with the key kept apart from it.

A stolen disk shows only scrambled data.

What can leave, and when

Life Is works without the internet. A few things reach outside, and each one is off until you turn it on (importing a recipe has no switch: it acts only when you paste an address) and says exactly what it sends:

WhatSends
Barcode look-upsThe number under the barcode, and nothing else
The weatherThe town you type, then the place you pick, rounded to about half a mile
A recipe from a websiteA request for that one page
Car recall checksThe make, model and year of each car, to the U.S. traffic safety agency (NHTSA)
An AI assistantYour question, only when you ask it, to the model at the address you give
Reminders by ntfy, browser push or email (on a Life Is you run)Each morning's reminder, to the ntfy server you choose, your browser maker's push service, or your own mail account. See Upkeep
Your own mail account (on a Life Is you run)Only between Life Is and your mail provider
Calendar links you follow (on a Life Is you run)A request for the calendar, about once an hour
Photos from Immich, and media and book servers (on a Life Is you run)Only to the servers you name, with the key you give. Life Is only reads, and changes nothing there

All of them are listed in one place: Settings › Connections. The phone app can also show its own morning reminders, made on the phone, with nothing sent. The privacy policy has the full list.

Personal keys

A personal key lets one shortcut on your phone, like Siri, read or add a few things in your Life Is without signing in. Keys are off until you make one (Settings, Account & sign-in, Personal keys), work only on a Life Is you run, and are shown once: Life Is keeps only a scrambled form. Each key does only what you ticked, sees only what you can see, and never reaches guns or ammunition, measures, hidden gifts, money or mail. You can turn a key off or delete it at any time, and it stops working at once. Life Is keeps the name, the list of what it may do and the last day it was used, and nothing else about its use. Keys are not in your export. See Siri and Shortcuts.

Keeping a copy

In the phone app: Settings › Your data makes a backup file, locked with a passphrase you choose. Keep it wherever you like. Life Is makes no copies of itself anywhere.

On a computer you run: copy the data folder and the keys folder you chose when you installed it. Keep both together: one is no use without the other. Life Is also saves a copy of the database by itself before every upgrade.

Taking it with you

Export everything (Settings › Your data, under Import and export) makes one zip of it all. Recipes export as a zip on their own. The home inventory prints or saves as a PDF. A backup holds everything.

Letterboxd, in and out. On the Lists tab, From Letterboxd brings in the zip from Letterboxd's Export your data: watched days, watchlist, lists, half-star ratings and reviews. The files are read here, you see the counts first, and nothing is kept until you say so. Reading the same download again adds nothing. Any list can also be saved as a CSV that Letterboxd's importer takes (Make a Letterboxd file). Neither way sends anything to Letterboxd: you carry the file yourself. Your ratings are in the download of everything too. Films, music and books has the details.

Bring in from HomeBox. On Things, choose Bring in from HomeBox and pick the CSV that HomeBox exports. You see the kinds, places and tags first, and nothing is kept until you say so. Bringing in the same file again adds nothing. Your home says what is brought across.
