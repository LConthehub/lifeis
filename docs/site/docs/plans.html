Plans and projects

Both are in the busy section. Its tabs are Calendar, Routines, Projects, Plans, Holidays, Trips and Activities.

Plans

A plan is anything with a day and things that belong to it: a vacation with its menu, dishes and films, or one afternoon of sewing. A plan can be ahead of you, going on now, or already over. A day that has gone is fine: that is a memory, written down afterward.

On Plans, choose + New. Give it a name, a first day, a last day if it is longer, and a few words on what it is for. Then open it and add what belongs to it:

  • Checklist. Anything to do. Tick each off.
  • Food. Recipes from your kitchen. Choose + Add, pick a recipe, and say which day and meal it is for and how many it feeds. On the week's meals it counts toward the grocery list and the pans and dishes the meal needs. It moves if the plan moves. Or choose "Not on the week" to just note it on the plan.
  • Things to have ready. Things you keep, and how many are needed. If you are short, the plan says so.
  • To watch, hear or read. Titles from your collection.
  • Projects. Projects that belong to it.

For a plan still ahead, the page also shows a rough cost of the food, from the last prices you noted. It leaves out anything with no price, and says which. It does not take away what is already in the pantry.

Do it again makes a new plan from this one: the same food, things, titles and checklist with nothing ticked, and a new first day. Food that was on the week goes on the new week, the same days in. The old plan stays as it was, with its story and photos.

Looking back

When a plan is over, its page shows What happened: what was eaten, worked on, watched, read and heard on its days. Write How it went in your own words. Add photos from this device, which are made smaller and kept with the plan. If you run Life Is yourself and have connected Immich, you can also choose photos from it. They stay in Immich, and the plan only remembers which they are.

Holidays that come back each year and outdoor trips are plans too, with more built in. See Holidays and Outdoor trips.

Projects

A project is something you make or do: a dress, a shed, a garden bed. Open Projects and choose + New project. Give it a name, what kind of project it is (the hints change to suit it, such as screws for woodworking and zippers for sewing), where it is at, and a day you want it done by.

Where it is at is one of: An idea, Planned, Working on it, Paused, Finished, or Dropped.

A project page has:

  • What it uses. Add things you keep, with an amount. Once the project is planned, that amount is set aside so another project cannot take it. Leave the amount empty for something that is only used, not used up, such as a pattern or a tool. A line says what you are short of. Choose Used it when you take the amount out of your things. If you do not have it yet, add it as "Something I don't have yet".
  • Time on it. Write down each day you worked on it, for how long, and what you did. You can also add a film, show or album from your collection you had on meanwhile. Its total shows at the top.
  • What it costs. Add what you expect to spend, then mark each one Paid with what it came to. It shows what is planned, spent and left, and warns you if it is over. A paid cost goes into your spending. You can also save up for a project. See Money and budget.
  • Your gear. The things you keep that are for this kind of project, and where each one is.
  • Notes.

When you mark a project Finished, what it had set aside and not yet used comes out of your things.

Tag a project or a plan to find it again. See Tags.
