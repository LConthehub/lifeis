Outdoor trips

An outdoor trip is a plan that comes back each year. You turn on the parts you need: camping, fishing, hunting. Each year starts from last year's: the bring list, the menu, the seasons and your notes. Past years stay to look back on.

Places are only words you type. Nothing is looked up on a map or sent anywhere.

Trips are in the busy section, on the Trips tab.

Start a trip

  1. Choose + Add a trip and give it a name, such as "Lake weekend".
  2. Check the parts you want: Camping, Fishing, Hunting. For a hike or a boat day, leave them all off and use the plain trip.
  3. Set the first day and the last day.
  4. Choose Keep this trip private if you want. Left shared, the people in your house see its name, its days and what was caught. Hunting is never shown to anyone.

When the year is over, choose Start next year's trip. It starts from this year's: the same bring list with nothing packed, the menu on new days, the seasons a year on, and your notes. Check the new dates.

Every trip

  • License check. If you have fishing or hunting on the trip, Life Is looks in Licenses and tells you whether each one covers the days of the trip. It says so if you have none.
  • Bring list. Choose Add my camping gear (or fishing, or hunting) to fill it from your things, or add things one at a time, or type something that is not one of your things. Tick each as you pack it. Next year starts with the same list, nothing packed. The fishing gear comes from things of the kind Fishing tackle, and the hunting gear from the kind Hunting gear.
  • After the trip. Tick what came back and say what each piece needs: dry, clean, repair or replace. A repair becomes a job in Upkeep. A replacement goes on the grocery list.
  • What it cost. Adds up the spending you tag with the trip's name, around its days. Set a budget to compare. See Tags.
  • How it went. Write a note, and write what next year should remember, such as "Bring a second tarp." You see it at the top next year.

Camping

  • Campsite booking. Type the campground, the site, your confirmation number, and check-in and check-out days. Check-in and check-out show on the calendar. Next year remembers the campground and site, not the confirmation number.
  • Camp menu. Put recipes on the days and meals they are eaten. Then choose Put the menu on the grocery list. It fills the list, less what the pantry has. Life Is also checks that you have a cooler and a stove among your things, and tells you if a recipe needs more equipment than you have.

Fishing

  • The water. Name it in words.
  • Seasons. Type the dates from the rules you read: when a season opens and closes. Add how many days before opening day you want a reminder. Next year's dates start a year on, so check them.
  • Catch log. For each fish, write what it was, the day, the water, its length and weight, the lure from your tackle or in words, and whether you kept it. A kept fish can go To the freezer, which adds it to your pantry's freezer. Past years stay in the log, with each year's count and longest fish.
  • What worked. From your own log, the lures that caught fish, by water and season.
  • Tackle running low. Things of the kind Fishing tackle that are running low. One press puts them on the grocery list.

Hunting

Everything under hunting is private to you. Other people in the house see only the trip's name and days.

  • Seasons. The same as fishing.
  • Ammunition check. On the bring list, give ammunition a caliber, such as .308 or 12 gauge. Life Is finds ammunition of that caliber among your things and says whether you have enough for what you bring.
  • Harvest log. Write the animal in your own words, the day, the place in words, the tag number and the weight. Meat can go To the freezer.

Life Is has no records of firearms. Only the ammunition among your things is counted.
