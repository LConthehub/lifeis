Money and budget

The budgeted section is your money. Its tabs are This month, Income and bills, Goals and Year. It is private: only you see it, unless you share a spending category.

Life Is keeps track. It never moves any money.

Spending, by category

On This month, a category is a name and an amount for each month: Groceries, House, Fun. Add one, then add what you spend with + Spent: what it was on, how much, the day, and a category if it fits.

The top of the page shows the month's planned amount, what was spent, and what is left. Use the arrows to see other months. Money spent but put in no category is listed on its own line.

A category can be shared. When you add or change one, check the people who should see it. They see that category and what was spent in it, and they can add to it. They cannot change its amount, and they cannot see any other category. Everything else stays yours alone.

Bring in a statement

Choose Import a statement and pick the CSV file your bank or card lets you download (look for Download or Export on the activity page). You can also paste its words. The file is read on your own Life Is and sent nowhere. You see what was found before anything is kept:

  • Deposits, refunds and card payments are not spending, so they are left out. Check one only if it really was spending.
  • Lines already in Life Is are marked, so reading the same file twice adds nothing twice.
  • If the file does not say which lines are spending, Life Is takes a guess and tells you which way. You can switch it.

If you run Life Is yourself, you can also read your bank directly. See Reading your bank.

Spending from the grocery list

When you finish shopping, the receipt becomes a spending entry. See The kitchen. What you saved with sales and coupons shows on This month under "Saved at the store".

Spending by tag

Put a tag on what you spend (see Tags). A tag shows what was spent with it, with the tags inside it, for the month, for the year, and on the tag's own page. Each entry is counted once.

Income and bills

Open Income and bills.

  • What comes in. Add each source: a paycheck, a pension, a gift. Say how much and how often: every week, every two weeks, twice a month, every month, every year, or one time on a date. A first and last day are optional. For weekly and every-two-weeks pay, give a first payday and Life Is counts the real paydays in each month. Without one, each month gets the average. At the bottom it says what it all comes to in a month.
  • Bills. Bills are kept in Upkeep, so there is one list. Add one here and it is added to Upkeep as well. Open a bill to change it or mark it paid. A bill with no date yet is not counted.

The month at a glance

Under the categories, The month's budget adds it up:

  • Income coming in that month.
  • Bills due that month.
  • Planned spending. For a month still to come, this is the larger of what you planned in the categories and what was spent. For a month that is over, it is only what was spent.
  • Put into goals, if you saved toward any.
  • Left over, or Short by if it is below zero.

A bill you pay and also write down as spending is counted once, as the bill.

Money paid on projects is already in the spending. Money planned on projects but not paid yet is shown as "coming" and is not in the totals.

The year

Year shows twelve months side by side (a list of months on a phone) and the totals for the whole year. Use the arrows for other years.

Bills count from the month each was written down. For months still to come, spending is the larger of what you planned and what was spent. Months gone by count only what was spent.

Goals

On Goals, add a goal: what it is for, how much it takes, and a day to reach it if there is one. Then:

  • Put money in when you set some aside. Take money out when you use it.
  • Each goal shows how much is saved, how much is left, and, if it has a day, what a month needs to reach it. If the day has passed, it says so.
  • The money you put in this month shows in the month's budget.

Under each goal is a flat bar with plain words: "$120 of $400, on track", "behind by $40", "over by $15" or "done".

A goal with a day shows what this month asks. On the month's budget, press Set aside to check it off; that puts the money toward the goal. If you fall behind, the later months ask more, and it says so.

A goal can belong to a plan, a holiday, a trip or a project, or to nothing. Spending you tag with a plan's tag comes out of that plan's goal. The month leaves it out, so it is counted once.

When a plan is over, Life Is asks once where the leftover goes, or what covers a shortfall. Nothing moves until you answer.

Safe to spend is on the month's budget and on each plan. It is the month's income, less bills, less what goals ask this month, less what you have spent so far. Tap it to see the four lines.

This only changes what Life Is says is saved. It does not move any money at your bank.

A project can have its own goal. Open the project and choose Save up for this, or pick a goal you already have. When you mark one of the project's costs as paid, you can take the money from the savings.
