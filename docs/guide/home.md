# Your home

The **organized** section holds what you own, where it is, what it is for, and where it has been. It also holds the records that go with each thing, your pets, and a house manual for a sitter. Jobs that come round again are in [Upkeep](upkeep.html).

## Places

A place is a room, a shelf in it, a bin on the shelf. Write a whole path at once, with `›` or `>` between the levels: `Garage › Tool shelf › Red bin`. Levels that already exist are reused.

Wherever Life Is asks "Where it is", the list ends with **A new place…**, so you never have to stop to make one.

## Things, and kinds of things

You make your own **kinds** (Fabric, Tools, Board games) and give each the fields you care about: a fabric's width, a game's number of players. Ready-made sets get you started, and every part of them can be changed.

Each thing has a page of its own with a photo, where it is, what it is for, its records, and its history.

## Filling a place

Open a place and choose **Fill this place**. Then keep scanning:

| You scan | What happens |
|---|---|
| A book, film, album or game you do not have | It is looked up, added, and put here |
| Something you already have | It is moved here, and its history says where from |
| A blank Life Is sticker | It asks what the sticker is on, once |
| Another place's sticker | It switches to filling that place |
| A barcode nobody knows | It asks what it is, and keeps the barcode for next time |

No barcode? Type the name and press Enter.

Looking a barcode up asks an outside catalog, so it is off until you turn it on. It sends the number under the barcode, and nothing else.

## Stickers

Life Is prints small stickers with a code your camera reads.

- **Place stickers** go on a shelf, a box or a bin. Scan one to see what is inside.
- **Thing stickers** go on things with no barcode of their own. Print a sheet of blank ones, stick them on, and say what each is on when you scan it.

Only someone signed in to your Life Is sees anything. To anyone else a sticker is an address that asks them to sign in.

## History

Every thing remembers when it came in, each time it moved and where from, and each time it was used and what for. Renaming a room later does not rewrite the past.

## What it is for

A thing can belong to activities: the sewing machine is for Sewing, the drill for DIY and Home maintenance. **Activities** gathers the gear for each, the projects of it, and its upkeep.

## Packing

For moving house or putting things away, **Packing** makes numbered boxes in one go. Pack each by scanning, and search will tell you which box anything is in. A box unpacks into its new home in one step.

## Receipts, serial numbers and warranties

After a fire, a flood or a theft, an insurer asks what you had, what it was worth, and whether you can show it. These records answer all three, and build up as you use Life Is.

Open a thing and find its **Records** card. It can keep:

- when and where it was bought, and what it cost
- what it is worth now, if that is different
- its model and serial number
- when its warranty ends

Beside the records, keep the thing's papers: a **receipt**, the **manual**, a **warranty card**. Each is a PDF or a photo, up to 12 MB. A photo is made smaller before it is kept; a PDF is kept as it is.

Search finds a thing by its serial or model number, which is what a repair shop asks for on the phone. A warranty about to end appears in your [morning reminder](upkeep.html#what-comes-due) a month ahead, a week ahead, the day before and on the day, so there is time to claim.

### The home inventory

On the Things page, **Home inventory** lists everything room by room, with what each is worth, its serial number, and whether there is a photo and a receipt to show for it. It says how many things still have no value, so you know what is left to do.

**Print it, or save it as a PDF, and keep a copy somewhere other than home.** An inventory that burns with the house helps nobody.

## Pets

Each pet has a profile: what they are, their breed and birthday, their vet with a number you can tap to call, their microchip number, what they eat, and notes such as allergies. Add a photo, and note their weight from time to time: Life Is shows the change since last time.

A pet's **care** is upkeep that belongs to them. Pick from what suits their kind (heartworm prevention for a dog, the litter box for a cat) or add anything else. Say when it was last done and Life Is knows when it is next due. Care works like any other job in [Upkeep](upkeep.html): it shows in your morning reminder and on the calendar, and each time it was done is kept.

## The house manual

The house manual is what someone else needs to know to look after your home: a sitter, a guest, family, or you on the day something goes wrong. It has four parts, each with ideas to start from:

- **In an emergency:** where the water and gas shut off, the breaker box
- **Who to call:** the plumber, a neighbor with a key
- **How the house works:** the Wi-Fi, the thermostat, the alarm
- **Day to day:** trash days, plants, bedtimes

An entry can say where a thing is, using the places you already keep, and carry a phone number to tap. On a phone, under **Who to call**, tap **Choose from your contacts** to fill in a name and number. Only the one contact you tap is read, and Life Is asks for no access to your address book. Your pets appear by themselves, with their food and vet.

**Print it** and put it in a drawer. Or **share it by a link**, for a sitter. The link opens the manual on their phone with no sign-in. You choose how long it works: a day, a week, or until you turn it off. Making a new link stops the old one.

Mark an entry **private**, such as an alarm code, and it is never on the shared link. The shared page is plain: nothing on it can be changed, and it shows nothing else of your Life Is.
